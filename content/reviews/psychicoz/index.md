## How they keep it honest

This is where PsychicOz separates from the "apply and you're in" crowd.

**A real gate.** Readers submit written applications — experience, specialty, tools, preferred method — then pass identity and account checks, an interview on communication and boundaries, and sometimes a test reading. Not everyone gets through.

**Fourteen rules of ethics.** The code bans guaranteed predictions, fear-based pressure, cross-platform soliciting, asking for extra payments or gifts, and dressing a reading up as medical, legal, or financial advice. In plain terms: the scariest tricks of this industry are banned on paper.

**They watch after hire.** Verified feedback, account activity, complaint patterns, and compliance reviews feed ongoing monitoring. Warnings, limits, suspensions — the platform can act.

**Reviews come from paid sessions.** Eligible, verified readings only. That raises the signal-to-noise on the ratings you're reading.

**A satisfaction guarantee.** If a session goes sideways, you can file through the Resolution Center within a window — the homepage says 48 hours, the about page says 24. Check the current policy before you file. The platform even claims: if a reading brought no value, you won't be charged.

None of this guarantees every sentence lands. Reader fit still varies. A screening process filters out the worst actors; it doesn't install good judgment in the ones who pass. Which is why the free three minutes matter.

## What the three free minutes actually are

Most people burn them. They show up without a question, spend two minutes giving background, and hang up with the reader's warm voice in their ear and nothing concrete in their hand. Then they book ten minutes to find out what the three were for.

Three minutes is enough for one question and one answer. Not a story, not context, not the history of the relationship. One sentence.

Write it down before you connect. Out loud, in your own words, on paper. Not "what's going on with him" — that's an invitation to a fishing expedition. Try "he says he needs space and I don't know whether to wait or move on. What do you see." Specific questions get specific answers, and specific answers can be checked against what actually happens next.

Two things to watch during those three minutes:

**Does the reader spend your clock gathering material, or answering?** Some setup is legitimate — a reader needs to know who you're asking about. Two minutes of setup out of three is not setup. It's the audition running long on purpose.

**Do you feel pressure to continue?** A good three minutes ends cleanly. The reader says what they see and stops. If the sentence is "there's more here, you should book a full session to get the full picture," that's a sales move, and it's the oldest one in the category. Some readers do it without meaning harm. The effect on you is the same: you leave with an open loop and a card on file.

The free minutes are genuinely useful. They're just only useful if you arrive with something to test.

## Where transparent pricing still costs you

This is the part nobody puts in the review, and it's the reason people who pick the "honest" platform still end up unhappy.

Per-minute pricing hides the total. Not from you maliciously — from you structurally. When a session is $2.99 a minute, your brain files it under *cheap* and stops running the arithmetic. Fifteen minutes is $45. Twenty is $60. Two sessions a week for two months is somewhere north of $400, and not one of those individual charges felt like a decision.

The intro packages make this sharper, not softer. Ten minutes for $9.99 is priced like an impulse buy because it is one. Five of those across a bad month is $50 and zero resolved questions, because five different readers gave you five different versions and you now have a new problem: choosing between them.

None of this is a PsychicOz-specific failure. It's how the model works everywhere, including on the platforms with worse reputations. PsychicOz's contribution is that it removes the *hidden* costs. It can't remove the accumulated ones. That part is on you, and it's the part that actually hurts.

Do the multiplication before you connect, not after. Pick a number for the month. Write it down. The number is what makes it a decision.

## How to use it without it using you

Four rules. Cheap to follow, and they're the difference between a platform you'd recommend and one you'd warn people about.

**One question per session.** Two questions means neither gets answered properly and you book a third session to finish the second one.

**A written budget before you connect.** Not a vague intention. An actual number you've decided you're comfortable spending this month, kept somewhere you'll see it.

**Write down what was said, immediately.** Memory is generous to readings. It'll quietly edit the vaguer predictions into accurate ones after the fact. A note written in the first ten minutes after hanging up doesn't do that. There's a reason the [research on intuition and memory](/tarot/science-of-intuition/) keeps landing on the same finding: we're excellent at remembering hits and terrible at remembering misses.

**Wait 24 hours before rebooking.** If the urge to book again shows up the same night, it's usually the open loop talking, not the information.

One more, and it's the one that matters most. If you've reached the point where you can't tell whether your own version of events is correct, a reading will make that worse, not better. Outsourcing the tiebreak to a stranger — any stranger, on any platform, however well vetted — trains the exact muscle you need to rebuild. That pattern has a name and a [much better set of responses](/psych/relationship/gaslighting/), and none of them involve paying by the minute.
